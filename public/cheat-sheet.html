Sequence Risk Cheat Sheet

Ten precision-machining sequence risks that quietly destroy margin. Tape it to the estimating desk.

Every line below is a sequence assumption that a senior estimator catches on a careful Tuesday-night read — and that gets missed on a Friday rush. Run a 60-second check against this list before any RFQ leaves the estimating desk.

01 · Stress relief
Stress relief between roughing and finishing on long shafts

Long parts (L:D > 8:1) without an explicit stress-relief callout typically need one anyway. Skipping it = warpage after final machining = scrap. Confirm the customer's expectation before pricing.

02 · Heat treat sequence
Heat treat must precede finish grinding — never the reverse

Grinding a part that's then sent for heat treat distorts the ground surface. If the print shows "heat treat" after "grind," it's either a sequence error or the print expects pre-heat-treat soft grind + post-heat-treat finish grind (two grind ops). Verify which.

03 · Nital etch on aerospace bearings
Nital Etch is required after grinding on hardened aerospace bearings

Often called out in title-block notes or page 30+. Detects grind burn. Missing it on a quote = unbillable rework. NADCAP-certified processor required for aerospace work.

04 · Electroless nickel buildup
Threads must be cut undersize before Electroless Nickel plating

EN adds 0.0002"–0.0005" per side. Threads cut to nominal will fail the gage after plating. Same applies to bores, OD slip-fits, and pilot diameters. Calculate pre-plate dimensions or scrap the lot.

05 · Hard coat anodize Type III
Hard Coat Anodize Type III adds 0.0005"–0.001" per side, masked features need protection

Critical features (bores, threads, shafts) need pre-anodize machining compensation OR explicit masking. "Hard Coat per MIL-A-8625 Type III" usually implies Class 2 (dyed black) — confirm with print or customer.

06 · Passivation type
Passivation: Nitric vs Citric matters for downstream coatings

Nitric (per AMS 2700 Method 1) leaves residue that affects adhesion of subsequent paint or epoxy. Citric (Method 2) is preferred for parts that get topcoated. If the print just says "passivate," ask which.

07 · NADCAP for aerospace HT/NDT
Aerospace heat treat and NDT require NADCAP-certified processors

If the part is for AS9100/AS9120 customers, your heat-treat vendor must be NADCAP-certified for that process (HT, FPI, MT, etc.). Non-NADCAP vendors won't work for source-inspected jobs. Verify before quoting lead time.

08 · Deburr & break edge
"Break all sharp edges .005-.015" R" is a real operation, not a freebie

A part with 40 features needs 40 deburr operations. On medical, aerospace, or food-contact parts, manual hand-deburr can run 10–20 minutes per part — frequently unscoped in the original quote.

09 · First Article Inspection (FAI)
FAI/PPAP requirements add 1–2 weeks before production can start

If the print or PO references AS9102 (FAI) or PPAP, the first-piece must be measured, documented, and approved by the customer before any further production. Estimators routinely under-quote the lead time. Add the buffer to your delivery commitment.

10 · "Unless Otherwise Specified"
Title-block tolerances vs the actual GD&T on the print

UOS in the title block (e.g., ±0.005") is the default — but specific features may have tighter explicit GD&T. Don't assume the title block applies to every dimension. Read every callout. The expensive ones are usually buried.
